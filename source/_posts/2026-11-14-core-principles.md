Once you find the core principles of something, you can limit the search space of your exploits and reach your desired outcomes by applying different sets of actions, as long as those actions are aligned with the principles. The problem is when:

- the principles cover a very narrow spectrum of your goals space, so you become repetitive and boring by strictly following them
- some, or all of the principles are wrong, so the respective actions will consistently miss the target
- the right principles are ignored completely, so the results of any action are unpredictable

## Climbing the ladder

Consider one’s professional career. There are certain formulas inside a company that consistently allow you to hit desired objectives, and allow you to climb the corporate ladder. For example, if your company rewards objective impact to its topline business goals, then you already know that one of the *principles* you can foster is to align your work consistently with the business’ needs[^1].

On the other hand, if you happen to find a local maxima principle, such as the rules on how to optimize a given user flow, and then don’t abide by any other principle, you’ll become pigeon holed into a role or position. This is usually less stressful, more predictable, but also more boring, and could leave you in a pickle if the ground shifts beneath your feet, as it often does in today’s business / world needs.

## Find your principles

If you think you already found all the core principles you need, great. Exploit them to the max, but be careful to keep an eye outside your perceived realm of possibilities, in order to make sure you are not missing anything fundamental.

If you found a core of set principles, but chose to disregard them in favour of new explorations, have fun, but remember that you might be wasting your previous investments, and leaving high leverage outcomes on the table.

If you don’t have a set of principles yet, whatever the area, make an effort to find them.

<br/>

[^1]: If your company doesn’t reward behaviours and impact that translates into positive outcomes to the business’ topline objectives, but instead rewards unproductive politics and drama, you can still play that game to climb the internal ladder, but I would advise you to leave instead. No toxic culture is worth anyone’s time.
